Yotpo Pricing in 2026: What the Free Plan Hides, Where the Bill Starts, and Who Should Pay for It

Yotpo pricing in plain numbers: what the free plan leaves out, what each tier costs by order volume, what Loyalty adds, and which stores get their money's worth.

Yotpo pricing is hard to pin down for one reason: the number changes with your order count, and the pricing page shows it on a slider instead of in a table. You open the page, see $15 or $89 depending on where the slider sits, and leave without knowing what your store would pay.

Here is the short version as of October 2026. Yotpo Reviews is free up to 50 orders a month. The Starter plan runs from $15 to $129 a month, and Pro starts at $119 and costs $169 at 500 orders.

Add Loyalty and you add at least $199 a month. Above that, the price is a sales conversation.

Most owners who search for this have seen Yotpo in every "best reviews app" list and want to know whether it belongs in a small store's budget. For most stores in their first year, it doesn't, and the math below shows why. For one specific kind of store it does, and I'll describe that store too.

This post covers what the free plan leaves out, each paid tier by order volume, the Loyalty add-on, what Premium and Enterprise mean, and a cost table against Judge.me and Loox at 100, 500, 1,000, and 2,000 orders. If you want features instead of prices, start with how Judge.me, Yotpo, and Stamped compare.

Yotpo pricing in 2026, in one table

Yotpo sells reviews as five plans. Two have public prices, two are quoted by sales, and one is free. These figures come from Yotpo's Reviews pricing page, checked in early October 2026.

Plan Price a month Monthly orders What it adds
Free $0 Up to 50 Automatic review requests, email templates, reviews shown on your store
Starter $15 to $129 Up to 1,000 Photo and video reviews, Google rich snippets, Google Shopping, carousel, no Yotpo branding
Pro From $119 ($169 at 500 orders) Scales with volume Google Seller Ratings, custom questions, smart sorting, community Q&A
Premium Custom quote Custom Translations, multi-store management, a dedicated success manager
Enterprise Custom quote Custom Omni-channel collection, custom API setup, security and compliance support

All prices are in US dollars and billed monthly. The thing to notice is the middle column. You don't choose a Yotpo price. Your order count chooses it for you.

What does the Yotpo free plan hide?

Nothing is hidden in the strict sense. It is all in the support docs. It just isn't on the pricing card, and it changes what "free" means.

The 50-order cap. The free plan covers stores with up to 50 orders a month. Yotpo's Free and Starter plans FAQ says that if you pass your order limit for the month, your review request emails are capped, and you are asked to upgrade to keep collecting. So the month your store has its best run is the month the requests slow down.

The email ceiling. The same page lists a limit of 500 outgoing review emails on the free plan, reminders included. New accounts also get 200 Kickstart credits in the first month, which are extra requests sent to past customers.

No photos, no stars in Google. Photo and video reviews, Google rich snippets (the star ratings under your search result), and Google Shopping all start on Starter. So does removing the Yotpo branding from your widgets.

That last point is the one that matters. Stars in search results are the main SEO reason to collect reviews at all, and Judge.me includes them on its free plan with no order cap. Yotpo's free plan works as a trial. It isn't a place to stay.

How much does Yotpo cost as your orders grow?

Yotpo pricing is based on your monthly orders, not the reviews you collect. A store with 400 orders pays the 400-order price whether ten customers leave a review or two hundred do.

Starter: $15 to $129 a month

Starter begins at $15 a month for the smallest paid tier. Yotpo's page shows $89 for up to 500 orders and states that Starter tops out at $129 a month at 1,000 orders. Yotpo lists no Starter price above 1,000 orders, so past that point expect to move to Pro.

The steps in between sit on the slider. The 100-order step was reported at about $24 a month in September 2026. That figure is not printed on the page itself, so set the slider to your own order count before you budget.

Pro: $119 and up

Pro starts at $119 a month for the smallest stores and is $169 for up to 500 orders. It adds Google Seller Ratings, custom questions in the review form, smart sorting, and community Q&A. Higher steps were reported at about $269 for 1,000 orders and about $299 for 2,000, again from the slider and not from the printed page.

What happens when you pass your order limit

On Free and Starter, review request emails are capped once you pass the month's order limit. On Pro, Yotpo says you can either turn on a "reviews booster" that keeps requests going for an added fee, or upgrade and pay the prorated difference.

Either way, a good sales month raises your reviews bill. Plan for this before Black Friday, because November order counts can push a store up a tier for a single month.

Annual billing, and where the charge shows up

Yotpo's Shopify App Store listing offers annual billing at $144 a year on entry-level Starter (20% off) and $1,068 a year on entry-level Pro (25% off). The saving is real, but you are locked to a tier for a year while your order volume is still moving.

The same listing notes that charges may be billed by Yotpo separately from your Shopify invoice. Shopify's own help page on app charges confirms that external charges don't appear on your Shopify bill, and that refunds are the app developer's decision. Check your card statement, not just Shopify.

The add-on that doubles the bill: Loyalty

Yotpo is not one product, and Yotpo pricing is not one number. Reviews is one line on the invoice, and Loyalty and Referrals is another. A store running both pays two subscriptions.

Loyalty has a free plan with a basic points program. The first paid tier is Pro, and Yotpo's Loyalty pricing page puts it at $199 a month with your first 500 orders included. After that you pay per order: $0.20 for orders 501 to 1,000, and $0.10 for orders 1,001 to 3,000.

Worked out, Loyalty Pro costs $199 at 500 orders, $299 at 1,000, and $399 at 2,000. Put that next to Reviews Pro at $169 and the reviews app is now the smaller half of the bill.

Yotpo also sells the two as a bundle and says it costs less than buying each one separately, with bigger savings on higher tiers. The bundle's Pro card starts at $368 a month for 100 to 599 orders, which matches $169 plus $199. Compare the bundle quote with the two separate prices for your own order count before you assume a discount.

What happened to Yotpo SMS and Email?

They are gone. Yotpo retired its Email and SMS products on December 31, 2025 and pointed customers to Attentive and Omnisend. Any pricing guide that still lists Yotpo SMS rates is out of date.

In 2026 the lineup is Reviews, Loyalty and Referrals, and a newer AI visibility product called Discover, which is custom-priced. If you wanted Yotpo because it put reviews, loyalty, email, and SMS under one roof, half of that roof no longer exists.

What do Premium and Enterprise mean?

They mean a quote, a contract, and a named person. Neither plan has a public price. Premium adds widget and email translations, multi-store management, and dedicated onboarding and success managers. Enterprise adds omni-channel collection, a custom API setup, and security and compliance support.

Yotpo says it offers month-to-month plans as well as annual contracts for brands that want extra service. On a quoted plan, assume annual unless told otherwise. Before you sign, ask four things in writing:

  • How long is the term, and what is the renewal price?
  • What happens to the price if your order volume drops?
  • Can you downgrade mid-term?
  • Which plan keeps the verified badge on reviews you import from another app?

For a store doing under $500K a year, these plans are overkill. They are built for brands with a team to run them.

Yotpo pricing vs Judge.me and Loox at 100, 500, 1,000, and 2,000 orders

Here is how Yotpo pricing compares for the same store at four order volumes. Figures marked "about" are the slider steps described above. The "Pro plus Loyalty" column is Reviews Pro and Loyalty Pro bought separately.

Orders a month Yotpo Starter Yotpo Pro Pro plus Loyalty Judge.me Loox
100 About $24 About $119 About $318 $0 or $15 $49.99
500 $89 $169 $368 $0 or $15 $99.99
1,000 $129 About $269 About $568 $0 or $15 $199.99
2,000 Not listed About $299 About $698 $0 or $15 $299.99

Judge.me is flat: free, or $15 a month for the Awesome plan, at any order count. The full breakdown is in the Judge.me pricing post.

Loox's free plan covers a store's first 500 orders in total, not 500 a month, so it isn't in the table. The Convert plan is $49.99 with 300 monthly orders included and $50 for each extra 300, and Unlimited is $299.99. I walk through those jumps in the Loox pricing post, and the feature side is in Loox vs Yotpo.

At 500 orders a month, a year of Yotpo Starter is $1,068. A year of Judge.me Awesome is $180. The $888 difference is what you'd be paying for Yotpo's widgets and brand name, because the core job (requests, photos, stars in Google) is covered by both.

Who should pay for Yotpo?

My rule with clients is simple. Yotpo pricing makes sense when a store is on Shopify Plus or past roughly $500K a year, and it wants reviews and loyalty from one vendor with one account manager.

Pay for Yotpo if most of these are true:

  • You want reviews and a loyalty program from one vendor, on one dashboard.
  • You sell through retailers and need reviews syndicated to their sites.
  • You have someone on the team whose job includes working the reviews dashboard.
  • A $400 to $700 monthly line doesn't change your margin.

Skip it if your store is under about $200K a year. At that stage Judge.me's free plan does the job, and if a free tool fits, I recommend the free tool. If what you want is photo-first widgets, Loox is the closer match. The four-app comparison sorts all of this by revenue band.

How to leave Yotpo without losing your reviews

"It got too expensive" is the most common reason stores leave, and the fear is always the same: losing the reviews. You won't, if you do it in this order.

  1. Export first. In the Yotpo admin, go to Manage, then Reviews, then Export. A CSV link arrives by email, and exporting is available on every plan, including Free. Q&A content is not included.
  2. Import into the new app. Judge.me's Yotpo migration guide covers the upload. It is free on every Judge.me plan and takes about five minutes to process.
  3. Re-add videos by hand. Videos are not imported by default, so note which reviews had them.
  4. Check your stars. Run a product URL through Google's Rich Results Test once the new widget is live.
  5. Cancel in two places. Cancel the plan on Yotpo's Billing page, then uninstall the app from Shopify. Uninstalling alone may not stop a charge billed outside Shopify.

Yotpo charges until the end of the billing period you are in, so time the switch near your renewal date. If you are also retiring products during the move, read how to migrate reviews when you discontinue a product first, because orphaned reviews are easier to rescue before the old app is gone.

Wrapping up

Yotpo pricing comes down to three facts. The free plan stops at 50 orders and leaves out photos and Google stars. The paid plans rise with your order count, from $15 to $129 on Starter and from $119 up on Pro. And the bill that surprises people is usually Reviews Pro plus Loyalty Pro, which comes to $368 a month at 500 orders.

None of that makes Yotpo a bad product. It is a strong platform for a brand with the order volume and the staff to use it. It is the wrong first reviews app for a store in its first year, because the same core job costs $0 to $15 a month elsewhere.

So here is what to do this week. Look up your average monthly orders for the last three months in Shopify. Set Yotpo's slider to that number and write down the Starter and Pro price. Then add 50% to your order count and check again, because that is your bill if things go well.

If the second number still looks reasonable against what the reviews are earning you, Yotpo is a fair choice. If it doesn't, you have your answer, and moving is a one-afternoon job. I've spent twelve years watching small stores pay for software sized for a company they aren't yet. Reviews are one of the few places where the cheap option is also a good one.

Frequently asked questions

If you're still unsure after reading these, just send the question.

Is Yotpo free on Shopify? +

Yes, up to a point. Yotpo Reviews has a free plan for stores with up to 50 orders a month, and it covers automatic review requests and showing reviews on your store. Photo and video reviews, Google rich snippets, and removing Yotpo branding start on the paid Starter plan.

Does Yotpo charge by orders or by reviews collected? +

By orders. Yotpo pricing is set by how many orders your store gets each month, not by how many reviews come in. A store with 400 orders pays the 400-order price whether ten customers leave a review or two hundred do.

Is Yotpo billed through Shopify? +

Not always. Yotpo's Shopify App Store listing says external charges may be billed by Yotpo separately from your Shopify invoice. Check both your Shopify bill and your card statement, and cancel on Yotpo's Billing page as well as uninstalling the app.

Can I cancel Yotpo at any time? +

Monthly plans can be cancelled from the Billing page in the Yotpo admin. You are still charged until the current billing period ends, and annual plans run to the end of the term you paid for. Custom plans are cancelled through your Customer Success Manager or Yotpo support.

Is Yotpo more expensive than Judge.me or Stamped? +

Compared with Judge.me, yes, once you are past Yotpo's $15 entry tier. Judge.me is free or a flat $15 a month at any order count. Compared with Stamped it is close on the Starter plan: Stamped lists $23 a month up to 200 orders, $99 up to 1,000, and $199 up to 5,000, while Yotpo Starter is $89 at 500 orders and $129 at 1,000.

Does Yotpo still offer SMS and email marketing? +

No. Yotpo retired its Email and SMS marketing products on December 31, 2025, and pointed customers to Attentive and Omnisend. In 2026 Yotpo sells Reviews, Loyalty and Referrals, and an AI visibility product called Discover.
