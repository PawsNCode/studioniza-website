Judge.me vs Stamped: Which Reviews App Fits Your Shopify Store?

A side-by-side of the two value-tier Shopify reviews apps: real 2026 pricing, widget speed, Google Shopping support, loyalty add-ons, and how hard it is to switch.

If you're comparing Judge.me vs Stamped, you've probably already done the first round of elimination. Yotpo and Okendo priced themselves out of your bracket, and now you're down to the two apps that small Shopify stores actually shortlist. Good instinct. These are the right two finalists for most stores under $1M in revenue.

The problem is that most comparisons of these two apps read like feature checklists written by someone who has never had to pick. Both collect reviews. Both send request emails. Both show stars. A checklist won't tell you that one of them removed its free plan in 2025, or that the price gap between them widens from $8 a month to $184 a month as your store grows.

I recommend reviews apps to Shopify clients for a living, and Judge.me is my default for stores doing under $200K a year. But that's a default, not a rule, and Stamped genuinely earns its price for a specific kind of store. This post lays out where each one wins: pricing as your order volume grows, review collection and widgets, page speed, Google Shopping and star snippets, the loyalty question, and what switching actually involves. If you want the wider field first, the full four-app comparison covers Loox and Yotpo too. This one goes deeper on the head-to-head you're actually deciding.

The short answer, before the details

Pick Judge.me if you're a new or growing store that needs reviews to work well without a monthly bill that climbs with your orders. The free plan is genuinely complete, the paid plan is $15 a month flat forever, and the app does the one job it exists to do.

Pick Stamped if you're consistently past 200 orders a month, you want reviews and a loyalty program from one vendor, or you're on Shopify Plus. Stamped has repositioned itself as a retention platform for mid-market brands, and at that stage its extra tooling starts justifying the extra cost.

The Shopify App Store numbers back up how differently these two are used. Judge.me holds a 5.0 rating across more than 35,000 reviews, mostly from small stores praising the free plan and the support speed. Stamped sits at 4.7 across roughly 3,400 reviews, with a longer feature list and a customer base that skews larger. Neither number means one app is better. They mean the apps are built for different stores, which is the whole point of this comparison.

Judge.me vs Stamped pricing: a flat $15 against tiers that climb

This is the section that decides it for most readers, so let's be precise.

Judge.me has exactly two plans. The Forever Free plan includes unlimited review requests, unlimited orders, photo and video reviews, and SEO rich snippets. The Awesome plan costs $15 a month flat, no matter how many orders you process, and adds branding removal, Q&A, product grouping, Google Shopping syndication, and the AI features. There's a 15-day free trial of Awesome if you want to test the paid features before committing.

Stamped restructured its pricing in 2025 and removed both the free plan and the free trial. Its Shopify App Store pricing now starts at $23 a month for stores doing up to 200 orders, jumps to $99 a month at the 1,000-order tier, and reaches $199 a month at 5,000 orders. Above 20,000 orders you're on a custom plan. Meanwhile, Stamped's own pricing page quotes website plans starting at $199 a month for a single product, which tells you where the company is pointing its business: upmarket.

Two things in that table deserve a closer look. First, the tier jump. A Stamped store doing 201 orders a month pays the same $99 as a store doing 1,000. If your volume sits just above a tier boundary, you're paying for headroom you don't use. Second, the trial gap. With no free plan and no trial, Stamped asks you to pay before you've seen the widgets on your own theme. For a new store deciding between the two, that alone is a reason to start with Judge.me and switch later if you outgrow it.

Where Stamped earns its price: review collection and widgets

Now the honest other side. Stamped is not just charging more for the same thing. Three features genuinely separate it.

In-email review forms. Stamped's standout feature lets customers write the review, add photos, and submit, all inside the request email itself, without clicking through to your site. Stamped reports this doubles the number of reviews its merchants collect compared with redirect-based requests. That's a vendor claim, so hold it loosely, but the logic is sound: every click you remove from the submission path raises the completion rate. Judge.me's request emails link out to a review form instead.

Deeper analysis tools. Stamped includes NPS surveys, sentiment analysis that flags reviews where the star rating doesn't match the written text, and photo categorization. Useful if someone on your team will actually read those reports. A solo founder rarely does.

Wider syndication. Beyond Google and Meta, Stamped distributes reviews to major retailers like Walmart and Target through Bazaarvoice, and syncs with the Shopify Shop app. If you sell wholesale into big retail, that matters. If you sell from one Shopify storefront, it doesn't.

Whichever app sends the request, timing moves the collection rate more than the form does. I've covered when to send the review request separately, and the short version is that the delay after delivery matters more than which app fires the email.

What do the widgets cost you in page speed?

Every reviews app adds JavaScript to your product pages, and product pages are where your Core Web Vitals matter most. So this question is fair, but the honest answer is less dramatic than the comparison posts suggest.

Both apps load their widgets asynchronously, which means neither should block your page from rendering. The practical difference is bundle size. Judge.me ships one of the lighter widget payloads in the category because it only does reviews. Stamped ships more JavaScript because it's a bigger platform, and more of its display features load on the product page.

Rather than trusting anyone's benchmark, including mine, test it on your own theme. Run your product page through PageSpeed Insights before installing, install the app, then run it again. Look at total blocking time and INP, not just the headline score. Themes handle third-party scripts differently, and a widget that behaves on one theme can drag on another. I walk through what those numbers mean in the post on Core Web Vitals and the INP fix most themes get wrong.

The takeaway for this comparison: speed is a real but small point in Judge.me's favor. It shouldn't decide the choice on its own.

Google Shopping and star snippets: closer than the marketing suggests

Stars in Google results come in two forms, and the two apps handle them more similarly than their marketing implies.

Product rich snippets are the stars under your product pages in organic search. Both apps support them. Judge.me injects the structured data automatically, on the free plan included. Stamped supports them too, but its main review widget requires the JSON-LD code to be installed manually, with support assisting on paid plans. Not a dealbreaker, but a setup step Judge.me doesn't ask of you. I've written a full walkthrough on how star ratings show up in search results if you want the mechanics.

Google Shopping product ratings put stars on your Shopping listings and ads. Both apps syndicate reviews there: Judge.me on the $15 Awesome plan, Stamped as a Certified Google Partner on its paid tiers.

Do you need a Certified Google Partner?

Stamped's partner status adds one thing Judge.me doesn't offer: Google Seller Ratings, the stars that appear on your text ads. Before you weigh that heavily, check the entry bar. Seller Ratings require at least 100 eligible reviews over the past 12 months with a 3.5-star average or higher. A first-year store doing 50 orders a month won't clear that bar for a while, which means the feature you'd be paying toward sits unused. For the stars most stores actually get, product snippets and Shopping ratings, the two apps end up in roughly the same place.

Is Stamped's loyalty add-on worth paying for yet?

The strongest argument for Stamped is consolidation: reviews, loyalty points, referrals, and VIP tiers from one vendor, with customer data flowing between them. That's a real operational win for a brand with repeat-purchase volume and someone managing retention.

Check the price of that win, though. Loyalty is a separate Stamped subscription, listed at $299 a month on the Shopify App Store, with a 20 percent discount when you bundle products. A store running Stamped Reviews at $99 plus Loyalty would spend more per month on those two apps than many new stores spend on their entire tool stack.

Judge.me doesn't run a loyalty program at all. It integrates with dedicated loyalty tools like Smile and LoyaltyLion, so you'd stack a second app when the time comes. That's less tidy than one platform, but it means you pay for loyalty only when you actually launch loyalty.

My honest position: a store in its first year or two has no business paying for a loyalty module. Loyalty programs work when you have repeat-purchase behavior to reward, and most new stores are still solving first-purchase trust. Pay for the features you'll use this quarter, not the ones on the roadmap in your head. If you reach the stage where consolidated reviews-plus-loyalty makes sense, you'll be doing the order volume where Stamped's pricing makes sense too. Those two moments tend to arrive together.

How hard is it to switch between Judge.me and Stamped?

Quick answer: not very, and knowing that should lower the stakes of this whole decision.

Both apps export reviews as CSV files, and both have import paths from the other. Moving from Stamped to Judge.me is the smoother direction: Judge.me has a dedicated Stamped import under Settings, so you export from Stamped, upload the file, and the reviews map across. Two caveats worth knowing before you switch: video reviews don't import automatically and need to be re-uploaded manually, and imported reviews may not carry verified status into feeds that require verification data.

One migration scenario matters more than app switching, and it's the one almost nobody plans for: what happens to reviews when you discontinue a product. Retire an old SKU without moving its reviews and you orphan every trust signal that product earned. Judge.me's product grouping handles this natively, which is a quiet but real reason it's my default recommendation. I've written a full guide on how to migrate reviews when you discontinue a Shopify product, and the process is far cleaner when your app supports grouping out of the box.

The practical conclusion: you are not marrying either app. Start with the one that fits your current stage, and switch with an afternoon of work if your stage changes.

Wrapping up

Here's the whole comparison in three sentences. Judge.me wins on price at every order volume, includes photo and video reviews for free, handles star snippets with no setup, and stays $15 a month forever. Stamped earns its higher price with in-email collection, deeper analysis, retail syndication, and a loyalty platform, all of which matter most to stores past 200 orders a month with retention on the agenda. For stores doing under $200K a year, Judge.me remains the right default, and it's the one I set up for clients at that stage.

If you're still unsure, let the free plan decide. Install Judge.me free, run it for 60 days, and watch your collection rate and your product page speed. If you find yourself wishing for in-email forms, NPS data, or a loyalty program, you've just learned you're a Stamped store, and the migration is a CSV file away. Most stores never reach that wish.

Whichever app you pick, the app is maybe 20 percent of the outcome. The request timing, the email copy, the widget placement, and how you respond to the reviews you get carry the other 80. The store with 40 reviews and thoughtful replies outperforms the store with 400 reviews and silence more often than you'd expect.

Frequently asked questions

If you're still unsure after reading these, just send the question.

Is Judge.me really free forever? +

Yes. The Forever Free plan includes unlimited review requests, unlimited orders, and photo and video reviews, with a small Judge.me badge on the widgets as the trade. You only pay $15 a month if you want the Awesome plan extras like branding removal, Q&A, and Google Shopping syndication.

Does Stamped have a free plan in 2026? +

No. Stamped removed its free plan in 2025, and its Shopify App Store pricing now starts at $23 a month for stores doing up to 200 orders a month. There is no free trial either, so you commit to a paid plan before testing the app on your store.

Can I move my reviews from Stamped to Judge.me without losing them? +

Yes. You export your reviews from Stamped as a CSV file, then use the dedicated Stamped import inside Judge.me to bring them across. Text and photo reviews carry over cleanly, while video reviews need to be re-uploaded manually after the import.

Which app should a brand-new Shopify store pick, Judge.me or Stamped? +

Judge.me, in almost every case. A new store has low order volume and tight margins, and the free plan already covers review requests, photos, videos, and star snippets. Revisit the judge.me vs stamped question once you consistently pass 200 orders a month.

Is the Judge.me Awesome plan worth $15 a month? +

It is once you run Google Shopping ads or split product variants into separate listings. Shopping syndication puts your star ratings in product ads, and product grouping pools reviews across variant listings. Before that point, the free plan does the job.

Does Stamped work with Shopify Plus? +

Yes. Stamped is built for Shopify and Shopify Plus merchants, and its platform now leans toward larger brands, with website plans starting at $199 a month and features like multi-store review syndication. That bracket is where its pricing makes the most sense.
