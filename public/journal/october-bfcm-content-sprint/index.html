The October Content Sprint: 6 Posts to Publish Before BFCM That Can Still Rank in Time

If you didn't start holiday content in August, BFCM content in October still works, for a short list of formats. Here are the six worth publishing in the next three weeks, in order of how fast each one ranks.

If you're reading this in mid-October with no holiday posts written, you are in the majority. Most guides start the plan in August, and most small stores spent August doing everything else.

Here is the honest position. BFCM content in October can still rank, but only some of it. Black Friday 2026 is November 27, which is 45 days from the day this post went up. That is too short for a new buying guide to climb past established pages. It is long enough for a page that answers a question only your store can answer.

That distinction is the whole post. When I wrote the full BFCM content plan in August, it worked back 15 weeks and covered nine to thirteen posts. This is the October-only version: six pieces, in order of how fast each one ranks, with the title pattern, word count, schema, and internal links for each.

Two of the six are Shopify pages, not blog posts, and one is a refresh. Six pieces over three weeks is two a week, which is the pace I'd suggest to a small store in any month.

Why BFCM content in October still works (and what it can't do)

Time is the constraint, so start with what Google says about it. Its documentation on asking for a recrawl says crawling can take anywhere from a few days to a few weeks, and that asking again for the same URL won't make it faster. That is the wait to be read, not the wait to rank.

Ranking is a separate wait, and it depends on who else wants the same search.

What stopped being possible in September

Anything that competes with other websites needs months. A buying guide, a "best [product] for [use]" post, a brand comparison: these go up against pages that have been collecting links and clicks for years. Published now, they will be indexed by Black Friday and sitting on page four.

Write them in January, for next year.

Can a new page really rank in six weeks?

Yes, when the search includes your brand name or a fact only you hold. Nobody else can answer "does [your store] ship in time for Christmas" or "when does [your store] start its Black Friday sale." For those searches there is nobody to climb past. The page only has to exist, be indexed, and be linked from your own site.

The demand behind those searches is large. Adobe's 2026 holiday forecast, published September 28, puts US online spending at $275.1 billion for November and December, with Black Friday alone at $12.9 billion. The same forecast expects traffic from AI assistants to retail sites to rise 130 percent over last year.

That last number matters for a late start. An AI assistant answering a question about your store works from whatever pages it can read, and a short page with dated, plain answers is the easiest thing on your site to quote.

The six posts, in order of ranking speed

Here is the full list. "Ranking speed" means how soon a piece can show up for the searches it is built for, fastest first.

# Piece Format Word count Schema Built to rank for
1 Holiday shipping deadlines page Shopify page 400 to 700 FAQPage, shipping policy [brand] Christmas delivery, [brand] shipping deadline
2 Black Friday page refresh Shopify page 300 to 600 FAQPage [brand] Black Friday
3 Deals preview FAQ Blog post 600 to 1,000 Article, FAQPage [brand] Black Friday deals, sale questions
4 Last-order-date announcement Blog post 300 to 500 Article [brand] last order date for Christmas
5 Gift guide on last year's URL Blog post (refresh) 1,500 to 2,200 Article, FAQPage Gifts for [recipient] under $[price]
6 Returns over the holidays Blog post 600 to 900 Article, FAQPage, return policy [brand] holiday returns

The first four rank on your brand name, so they show up almost as soon as they are indexed. The fifth competes with other sites, which is why it only works as a refresh. The sixth is last because its readers arrive last, in late December.

Permanent reference information belongs on a page and dated updates belong on the blog. The full split is in Shopify blog vs page.

Posts 1 and 2: the two pages that rank in days

1. The holiday shipping deadlines page

This is the fastest of the six because the facts now exist. USPS published its 2026 holiday dates on September 22. For the lower 48 states, the send-by dates for Christmas delivery are December 17 for Ground Advantage and First-Class Mail, December 18 for Priority Mail, and December 19 for Priority Mail Express.

Your page does not repeat those dates. It converts them. Subtract your processing time and a day or two of buffer, then publish the date a customer has to order by. If you ship with FedEx or UPS, check their holiday pages before you finalize anything.

  • URL: /pages/holiday-shipping, with no year, so you reuse it every season.
  • Title pattern: Holiday Shipping Deadlines 2026: Order-By Dates at [Brand]
  • Length: 400 to 700 words: one lead sentence, one table, four to six questions.
  • Schema: FAQPage for the questions, plus shipping policy markup if your theme or developer can add it.
  • Links to add: announcement bar, cart page, footer, order confirmation email, and every made-to-order product page.

The structure and the update calendar are in the holiday shipping deadlines page walkthrough. If you only publish one thing from this list, publish this.

2. The Black Friday page refresh

If you ran a sale last year and kept the page, you are ahead. Open it, change the year in the title and the copy, put in this year's dates (November 27 to 30), and save. Google has recommended this for years: use one recurring URL for the event instead of a new one each time, publish it early, and link to it from your homepage.

If you never had one, create it today at a URL with no year in it. The search it needs to win is "[brand] black friday," and you are the only candidate.

  • URL: /pages/black-friday
  • Title pattern: Black Friday 2026 at [Brand]: Sale Dates and Early Access
  • Length: 300 to 600 words before the sale: dates, an email signup, category links, a short FAQ.
  • Schema: FAQPage only. Sale prices belong in product markup once the deals are live, not on this page.
  • Links to add: footer now, homepage and main navigation from early November.

Add review blocks from the products you plan to discount. If those are thin on reviews, the reviews before Black Friday plan has an October step that still applies. The rest is in one Black Friday page, every year.

Posts 3 and 4: the dated answers

These two are short blog posts. Shoppers ask the same handful of questions every November, and a dated post is the natural home for a dated answer.

3. The deals preview FAQ

A deals preview tells people a sale is coming and how it will work, without giving the discount away. You don't need final numbers to write it. You need the rules.

Write it as eight to ten questions with short answers: when the sale starts and ends, whether subscribers get early access, whether codes stack, what is excluded, whether you'll adjust the price on last week's order, and whether sale items can be returned.

  • URL: /blogs/news/black-friday-deals-preview, reused each year.
  • Title pattern: [Brand] Black Friday 2026: What's on Sale, When It Starts, and How It Works
  • Length: 600 to 1,000 words.
  • Schema: Article and FAQPage.
  • Links to add: the Black Friday page, the shipping deadlines page, the returns post, and the three collections most likely to be discounted.

Older guides get the schema wrong. Google stopped showing FAQ rich results on May 7, 2026, so the markup no longer changes how your listing looks. It is still worth adding, because it gives AI assistants clean question-and-answer pairs to read.

4. The last-order-date announcement

This is the smallest piece on the list: a news post that says, in its first sentence, the last day to order for Christmas delivery. It gives your emails, social posts, and chatbot one link to point at.

It ranks fast for the same reason the shipping page does, and it shows up in your blog feed with a date on it, which tells shoppers the information is current.

  • URL: /blogs/news/last-order-dates-christmas
  • Title pattern: Last Order Dates for Christmas 2026 at [Brand]: Order by December [Day]
  • Length: 300 to 500 words.
  • Schema: Article. No FAQ needed.
  • Links to add: the shipping deadlines page, the gift guide, and gift cards for anyone past the cutoff.

One rule: the page holds the full table, and the post states the headline date and links to the page. If a date changes, change it in both places on the same day.

Posts 5 and 6: the two that need the most runway

The gift guide needs runway because it competes with other sites. The returns post needs it because its readers arrive in the last week of December, long after you've stopped thinking about content.

5. The gift guide on last year's URL

A gift guide is the only piece here that goes up against publishers and bigger stores. In October, that only works if the URL already has history. A refreshed guide keeps its links, its age, and whatever rankings it earned last December. A new one starts from zero.

So the instruction is narrow: refresh, don't write. Swap out anything out of stock, update the prices, change the year in the title and the opening paragraph, and add two or three new picks. Then update the modified date, because you really did modify it. The refresh checklist covers the steps.

  • URL: whatever it was last year. Do not create a 2026 copy.
  • Title pattern: Gifts for [Recipient] Under $[Price]: The 2026 Guide
  • Length: 1,500 to 2,200 words, with 8 to 15 products.
  • Schema: Article and FAQPage.
  • Links to add: each product, the Black Friday page, the shipping deadlines page, and a link to the guide from your homepage in November.

What if there is no guide from last year? Write one and keep it narrow (one recipient, one price band). It probably won't rank on Google this season. It will give your emails and product pages something to link to, and next October it is the URL you refresh. The structure is in how to write a holiday gift guide.

6. The returns-over-the-holidays post

Gift buyers want to know one thing before they pay: if this is wrong, can the person I'm giving it to send it back in January? This post answers that in plainer words than a policy page does.

State the extended window with real dates (for example, anything bought from November 1 can be returned until January 31), then cover exchanges, gift receipts, return shipping, and final sale items.

  • URL: /blogs/news/holiday-returns
  • Title pattern: Holiday Returns at [Brand]: Extended Window for Gifts Bought From November 1
  • Length: 600 to 900 words.
  • Schema: Article and FAQPage on the post. Separately, a seasonal override on your store-level return policy.
  • Links to add: the policy page itself, the Black Friday page, the deals preview, and the shipping deadlines page.

That last schema item is one most guides skip. Google's return policy structured data supports a seasonal override, so a store can declare a longer holiday window for a set date range instead of rewriting its whole policy. One caution: if you have return settings in Merchant Center or Search Console, Google uses those over the markup, so change them there first.

What should you skip in October?

A late start makes it tempting to do everything at once. I'd leave these four alone until January.

  • New buying guides and comparisons. They need months, not weeks, so the work can't pay off this season.
  • Year-stamped URLs. A handle ending in 2026 is abandoned by next November. Put the year in the title and the copy, never the URL.
  • A batch of 20 AI-written holiday posts. Google ran another spam update from August 18 to 21, and publishing pages at scale that add little value is one of its standing spam policies. Twenty near-identical gift posts in a week is the shape that policy describes.
  • New pages in the last two weeks of November. By then, anything new is for email and social, not search.

All four are what most stores do under deadline, which is why the list above stops at six.

The three-week schedule: which post goes out when?

The publishing order is not quite the speed order. The two pages go first because every other piece links to them. The gift guide goes next because it needs the most time. The two dated posts go last because they depend on decisions you make last.

Week Dates Publish Why now
1 October 13 to 18 Holiday shipping deadlines page, Black Friday page Everything else links to these two.
2 October 19 to 25 Gift guide refresh, holiday returns post The guide needs the most time, and the returns window has to be settled early.
3 October 26 to November 1 Deals preview FAQ, last-order-date announcement Both wait on decisions you make last, and both rank within days.

After each piece goes live, do two things. Inspect the URL in Google Search Console and request indexing, once. Then add the internal links listed for that piece, because a page nothing links to gives Google little reason to come back.

If two pieces a week is more than you have, cut from the bottom of the schedule. The shipping deadlines page and the Black Friday page do most of the work.

From November 2 onward, stop writing new things. Update dates as carriers confirm them, swap the Black Friday page to live deals on November 27, and send people to what you've built.

Wrapping up

BFCM content in October is a smaller job than it sounds. You are not trying to outrank anyone. You are making sure that when a shopper searches your store's name plus "black friday," "shipping," or "returns," the answer is yours, current, and dated.

Three things to keep:

  • Publish what only you can answer. Brand-specific pages rank in days. Competitive guides don't rank in weeks.
  • Reuse URLs. A page gets stronger each year it stays at the same address.
  • Stop on November 1. After that, the work is updating and distributing, not writing.

The part I can't shorten for you is the deciding. The shipping page needs your real processing time, the returns post needs a window you're willing to honor, and the deals preview needs the rules of your own sale. That is an hour or two of thinking before any writing.

If this season's content doesn't rank the way you hoped, it is not wasted. Every piece sits at a URL you will refresh next October with a year of history behind it, which is a better starting position than the one you have today.

Frequently asked questions

If you're still unsure after reading these, just send the question.

Can a blog post published in October rank before Black Friday? +

Yes, if it targets a search only your store can answer, such as your shipping cutoff, your sale dates, or your holiday returns window. Those pages can show up within days of being indexed. A post that competes with other websites, such as a buying guide or a best-of list, usually needs months and should wait until January.

How many posts does a small Shopify store need for BFCM content in October? +

Six is the full list: a holiday shipping deadlines page, a Black Friday page, a deals preview FAQ, a last-order-date announcement, a refreshed gift guide, and a holiday returns post. If time is short, the shipping deadlines page and the Black Friday page matter most. Two finished pieces beat six half-written ones.

Should Black Friday blog posts on Shopify have the year in the URL? +

No. Keep the year out of the URL handle and put it in the title and the body copy. A handle without a year can be refreshed every season and keeps its links and history, while a year-stamped handle is abandoned twelve months later.

Is FAQ schema still worth adding to holiday posts? +

Yes, though not for the old reason. Google stopped showing FAQ rich results on May 7, 2026, so the markup no longer changes how a listing looks in search. It still gives AI assistants and other search engines clean question-and-answer pairs, which matters more each season as AI-referred traffic to retail sites grows.

What if my store has no gift guide from last year to reuse? +

Write one narrow guide for a single recipient and price band, and treat it as next year's asset. It is unlikely to rank on Google this season, but it gives your emails and product pages something useful to link to. Next October it becomes the URL you refresh instead of starting from zero.

What should I publish in November after the October sprint? +

Almost nothing new. Use November to update carrier dates, swap the Black Friday page to live deals on November 27, and send email and social traffic to the pages you already built. New pages published in mid-November rarely have time to be found through search.
