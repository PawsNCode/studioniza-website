Blog Post Ideas for a Clothing and Homewares Shopify Store: 40 Topics Mapped to the Season and the Sale

Ask most boutique owners what's on their blog and the answer is "new arrivals". That is an announcement. Nobody types "new arrivals" into Google hoping to find a shop they've never heard of, which is why those posts sit at zero visits.

If you're hunting for blog post ideas for a clothing store or a homewares shop, you're in the biggest crowd on the platform. Apparel makes up 27.0% of all Shopify stores and Home & Garden makes up 12.4%, the top two categories, according to Store Leads' September 2026 count. That is roughly 838,000 apparel stores and 384,000 home stores asking the same question.

The lists that rank for this search were written for fashion bloggers, not shop owners: outfit-of-the-day posts, celebrity style, giveaways. None of them says which product a post should sell or which month it should go live.

I know the broad version underperforms because I wrote one. My own list of 30 blog topics for any Shopify store sits at position 17.7 in search, because "any store" is too wide to win a niche question.

So this post is narrow on purpose: 40 topics for stores that sell clothes, home goods, or both, each with the question behind it and the product it should link to. Then a 12-month map, and the ten to write before Black Friday.

What should a clothing or homewares store blog about?

Write about the questions people ask right before they buy something you sell. For a clothing store that means fit, fabric, care, and what to wear it with. For a homewares store it means size, material, care, and how it looks in a room.

Every topic in this post passed a three-part test. If an idea of your own fails one part, park it.

  • A question someone types. "How to wash linen sheets" is a search. "Our spring mood board" is not.
  • A product it links to. If you can't name the product or collection the post points at, it may bring readers but it won't bring orders.
  • A month it peaks. Some questions are asked all year, and most swell in one season. Knowing which tells you when to publish.

The format matters less than the test. I covered the eight blog post formats separately if you want the word counts for each.

What to skip

Three kinds of posts fill the big lists and do little for a small store.

  • New arrivals and restock posts. That is email and Instagram content, and it is stale in three weeks.
  • Trend reports and celebrity style. You'd be competing with magazines that have whole teams, for readers who aren't shopping.
  • Lifestyle posts with no product in them. This is what most stores do at this stage: a lovely post about cozy autumn evenings that never mentions a single thing you sell.

The 40 blog post ideas for clothing and homewares stores, in five groups

Jump to a group: fit and care, styling and room guides, seasonal and gifting, behind the product, or comparisons and buying guides.

In each group, the first four topics are for clothing and the homewares blog ideas are the four that follow. Swap the bracketed words for your own products. If your store sells both, you have all 40.

Group 1: Fit and care guides (topics 1 to 8)

Start here. These are the questions that stop a sale or cause a return. An estimated 19.3% of online sales were returned in 2025, and Shopify's returns guide notes that apparel runs higher because fit is hard to judge online. A post that answers "how does this run" before checkout earns its keep.

When should each blog post go live? The 12-month map

Publish a seasonal post before the season, not during it. Search Engine Land's guide to SEO seasonality puts planning at three to six months before the peak, and notes that content needs roughly 8 to 12 weeks to show up in search. For a small store, I'd treat two months ahead as the minimum.

If your store is in Australia or New Zealand, shift the weather-led rows by six months and keep the gifting rows where they are. Christmas is still in December. It just needs linen instead of wool.

Topics that aren't on the map, which is most of the fit, care, and behind-the-product posts, are asked all year. To check any topic's real peak, look it up in Google Trends over five years.

Which ten should you write first before BFCM?

Black Friday 2026 is November 27. As I publish this, that is seven weeks away, which is tight. A post published now can be indexed within days, but it won't have months to climb.

So pick topics that pay off even with modest rankings, because you can link them from product pages, order emails, and your sale page. In this order:

  1. How your sizing runs (1). Link it from every product page. It answers the question that blocks the most orders during a sale.
  2. What size rug, or what size insert (7 or 8). The same job for homewares.
  3. A gift guide at a fixed price (17). Sale shoppers are buying gifts. Have it live by late October.
  4. Housewarming and host gifts (21). December is full of visits.
  5. Your strongest comparison (33 or 37). On my own site, comparison posts are the ones AI assistants cite most.
  6. How we pack fragile orders (32). Gift buyers worry about breakage more than price.
  7. A care guide for your best-selling material (2, 5, or 6). Link it in your order confirmation email.
  8. Party season outfits (20). Publish by early November.
  9. How to set a holiday table (22). It sells dinnerware as a set.
  10. Why we chose this fabric, or what goes into the price (26 or 27). When everything around you is 40% off, this is the post that explains why yours isn't.

Ten posts in seven weeks is one or two a week, which is the right pace. Two to three solid posts a week is the ceiling I'd recommend for any small store. If you're starting from an empty blog, the first ten posts for any new store covers the general order.

Schedule them instead of publishing in a burst. Shopify lets you set a future visibility date on a blog post, so you can write three in a weekend and release one a week.

Everything else on the seasonal map is an investment in next year. A summer linen post published in March 2027 has time to rank. One published in June doesn't.

Wrapping up

The honest answer to "what does a clothing store blog about" is: the same things your customers email you about. Fit, fabric, care, and what goes with what. A homewares store gets the same questions with different nouns.

Three things to take with you:

  • Run every idea through the test: a question someone types, a product it links to, and a month it peaks.
  • Write fit, care, and comparison posts before styling and story posts. They sit closest to the sale.
  • Publish seasonal posts two months early, and accept that this year's late ones are for next year.

The blog post ideas for a clothing store or homewares shop in this list aren't clever, and that's the point. What makes them work is your detail: your measurements, your photos, your maker, your own washing instructions. That part can't be copied from a list, mine included, and it takes real time. Budget three to four hours for a good post.

Pick one topic from Group 1 this week, write it, and link it from the product page it supports.

Frequently asked questions

If you're still unsure after reading these, just send the question.

How often should a clothing store post on its blog? +

Once a week is enough for most small clothing stores, and two to three posts a week is the most I'd recommend. Consistency matters more than volume, because one useful fit or care guide does more than five thin posts. If you can only manage two posts a month, make them the two questions customers email you about most.

Do blog posts help a clothing store sell, or do they only help SEO? +

They sell when each post links to a specific product and answers a question that was blocking the purchase. A sizing guide linked from a product page can help close orders even if it never ranks in Google. Posts with no product in them tend to bring readers who leave without buying.

Should a size guide be a blog post or a page? +

Use a page for the size chart itself, because it is permanent reference content that every product links to. Use a blog post for the longer explanation of how your sizing runs, with real measurements and photos. Then link the two to each other.

Can I use AI to write blog posts for my boutique? +

AI is fine for outlines and first drafts, but the details that make these posts useful have to come from you: your measurements, your fabric, your photos, and your care instructions. A generic AI post about linen says the same thing as a thousand others. Blog post ideas for a clothing store only work when the answer is specific to what you sell.

How long should a homewares blog post be? +

Most homewares posts work at 1,200 to 1,800 words. A single sizing answer, such as what size rug fits under a queen bed, can be shorter at around 800 words, as long as the number appears in the first paragraph. Comparisons and buying guides usually need the longer end of the range.

My store sells both clothing and homewares. Should I have one blog or two? +

One blog. Splitting it halves the number of posts in each and gives you two places to maintain. Use tags such as Clothing and Home so readers can filter, and let gift guides and seasonal posts cover both categories in the same post.
